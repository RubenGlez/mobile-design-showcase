New Version 1.0: six modes, any stack

The senior mobile designer your agent was missing.

mobile-design is an agent skill that shapes briefs, designs screens, critiques, refines, hardens, and adapts iOS and Android interfaces. It delivers decisions, not mockup filler: structure, tokens, states, copy, and motion that hold in native, cross-platform, or web code.

01 Shape mode

From a one-line request to a brief worth designing.

“Make an app for our ceramics studio.” Shape mode asks only the questions that would change the design, proposes an answer for each so you can just confirm, and stops at a brief. No pixels yet.

Asks
One round, at most three questions, each with the likely answer already stated.
Never asks
Hex codes, spacing, or anything a designer should decide.
Ends with
The smallest brief that settles the decisions, then stops for confirmation.
The prompt

Use mobile-design to plan an app for our ceramics studio members. Don’t design screens yet.

You

Make an app for our ceramics studio members.

Agent, in Shape mode

Reading this as a members’ utility for a shared studio, used between wheel sessions with clay on their hands, on iOS and Android. Three questions; I’ve assumed an answer for each.

  1. What do members most need from it?

    When their piece comes out of the kiln. The firing queue is the core, not class booking.

  2. In what state do they reach for it?

    A glance, one thumb, wet or muddy hands. A tablet on the studio wall shows the same queue.

  3. What is true here that no booking app could claim?

    Results are uncertain. Pieces crack and glazes surprise. Status must be honest, not cheerful.

Brief

Kiln, members’ app

Draft

Job and audience

Members checking on their pieces between sessions: one thumb, hands not clean. The studio’s wall tablet is a second surface for the same queue.

Outcome

Know where every piece is, from queued to loaded, firing, cooling, ready, or cracked. Success is never having to ask staff.

Direction

A calm workshop utility. Signature: the kiln shelf, with each member’s pieces drawn on the shelf they fire on.

States and ranges

No pieces yet. One to forty pieces per member. Firing delayed. A cracked piece: show the photo, say it plainly, link the reclaim policy.

Constraints

Large targets for wet hands. A notification when a firing ends. Readable from a metre away on the wall tablet.

Confirm the three answers to complete the brief.

02 Design mode

A transfer that never calls pending a success.

Solace is a fictional bank for people who move money rarely and worry when they do. The screen is built around one object, the settlement line, and it tells the truth at every step.

Read
A one-off transfer for an anxious, occasional sender, with a calm, exact interface, on iOS and Android.
Signature
The settlement line: approved, sent, arrived, each with a real time. Arrival is never claimed before the receiving bank confirms it.
States
Review, authenticating, sending, sent, arrived. Failed keeps the money visibly in your account.
System
Ink #14211D on bone #F6F4EF, 15.1:1. Accent #1E5B49 only on the one action, white label at 7.9:1. Dark mode mirrors every role.
The prompt

Design the transfer confirmation for Solace, a bank for occasional senders. iOS and Android, light and dark. Show every state through to arrival.

03 Critique mode

A review that tells you what to fix first, and why.

Relay’s support queue looks finished and still fails the people who use it. Critique mode reads it like a senior reviewer: first impression, specificity, five lenses in order, then findings ranked P0 to P3, each with its fix.

First impression
It communicates a dashboard template. The eye lands on the greeting, the purple cards, and the emoji, not on who is waiting.
Specificity
Interchangeable. Swap the logo and this is any SaaS app. The product’s real object, the queue, starts below the fold.
Instruments
Trunk test 3 of 6. Cognitive load high. Goodwill falls from 70 to 35 across the flow.
Verdict
Don’t ship. One P0 locks people out of closing tickets.
The prompt

Run a full mobile-design critique of Relay’s support queue screen, iOS. We track rule IDs, so include them.

04 Refine mode

Same product, three honest directions.

Refinement works inside an identity instead of replacing it. Switch Levain’s bake screen between quieter, bolder, and distilled. Each keeps the content and the function, and changes only what its sub-mode allows.

Read
A bake companion for home bakers mid-recipe, flour on their hands, on iOS.
Signature
The rise curve: how far the dough has grown against the target.
Rule
A refinement keeps the identity. A redesign treats it as evidence. Never split the difference.
The prompt

This Levain bake screen feels busy. Give me quieter, bolder, and distilled versions so I can compare. Keep it Levain.

05 Harden mode

A ride log that tells the truth when everything fails.

Harden mode designs the states a demo never shows. Trailguard keeps recording through lost signal, denied location, a dying battery, and the system closing the app, and it never draws a route it didn’t measure.

Risk preflight
Background location: if denied, the ride continues with time only. No network: the log stays on the phone and syncs later. Low battery: fewer route points, and the rider is told the trade-off.
Signature
The measured route. Solid where GPS recorded it, a labelled gap where it didn’t, never an interpolated guess.
Platform
Android first, with Material 3 components, predictive back, and an ongoing notification while recording.
Light, on purpose
Chosen for the use scene, direct sunlight on a handlebar, not for the category.
The prompt

Harden Trailguard’s live ride screen on Android. Cover no signal, location off, low battery, and the app being killed mid-ride.

06 Adapt mode

One checkout, every platform, language, and window.

Adapt mode translates idioms instead of transplanting them. Maison North’s delivery step moves between iOS and Android grammar, survives German’s long words and Arabic’s right-to-left reading, and becomes two panes on a tablet.

Platform
iOS: inset grouped fields, back labelled with the previous screen, centred title. Android: outlined fields, top app bar, leading title, predictive back.
Language
Mirrored layout and direction-aware arrows in Arabic, Arabic-Indic digits from the locale, prices formatted per locale, never concatenated.
Window
Compact: one column, total pinned above the action. Expanded: form and order summary side by side.
The prompt

Adapt Maison North’s delivery step for Android and for tablets, and localise it for German and Arabic.

How the skill thinks before it draws.

The process runs silently. What reaches you is one line of reading and inferences, then the design.

  1. 1Context

    Reads your DESIGN.md, brief, existing screens, and anything that reveals brand or tokens.

  2. 2Design read

    Names the category, audience, direction, and platforms, plus the screen’s single job.

  3. 3Ask once, or infer

    At most three questions with likely answers, or the inferences stated in one line.

  4. 4Plan and mirror test

    Would a neighbouring product get this exact plan? Then it’s a default, and one decision changes.

  5. 5Produce

    Loads the craft floor and only the references the task needs.

  6. 6Verify

    Every state, large text, dark mode, the narrowest width. Says what it couldn’t check.

The floor. Never waived, whatever the brief.

Each item is checked on the result, not intended.

  • Targets of at least 44pt on iOS and 48dp on Android
  • WCAG AA contrast, in light and in dark
  • Text follows the system size and reflows, never clips
  • Nothing essential under the notch, bars, or keyboard
  • Every control has a name, role, and state
  • Every gesture has a visible alternative
  • Loading, empty, error, and populated, each with a way out
  • Irreversible actions are explicit and protected
  • Motion has a purpose and a Reduce Motion path
  • No invented metrics, reviews, or dark patterns

Version 1.0 works with any stack.

The skill no longer ships framework code. Designs are specified in pt and dp, colour roles, type roles, states, and behaviours. When you’re implementing, your agent reads your project and translates the decisions into its own vocabulary.

  • SwiftUI
  • UIKit
  • Jetpack Compose
  • React Native
  • Flutter
  • Mobile web
